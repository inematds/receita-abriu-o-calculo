How much of your money goes to the new tax? The Federal Revenue opened the books

CBS, the federal tax that will be built into the prices of what you buy, begins in January 2027. In November, the Federal Revenue released the program it uses to do this calculation. We ran the same purchase through it twice, and got R$ 56.76 and R$ 72.88. The difference is the tax rate, which the Senate still has to decide.

In one sentence

Brazil’s Federal Revenue Service opened to everyone the program that calculates the new taxes in the tax reform: CBS (federal, replacing PIS and Cofins), IBS (for states and municipalities), and the Selective Tax (which applies to drinks, cigarettes, and other products). Before, this calculation was buried in company systems, and outsiders couldn’t see it. Now anyone can download, run, and check it. This page explains what happened in November, shows the same purchase calculated at the two rates under consideration, and tells you what it means for you. Just read on; if you want to run the calculation, you’ll find the steps under “Check it yourself”.

The two calculations

The same purchase, R$ 16.12 apart

We took the example Brazil’s Federal Revenue Service publishes in the program documentation and ran it twice, changing just one number: the CBS rate.

🧾 Calculation A: CBS at 8.8% → R$ 56.76

This is the rate the Federal Revenue used in its documentation example. It’s for testing the program; it is not a proposed rate. The total matches the figure in the official documentation.

🧾 Calculation B: CBS at 11.3% → R$ 72.88

This is the benchmark rate the TCU (Federal Court of Accounts) calculated for 2027 and submitted to the Senate. It has not been approved yet. At this rate, CBS on the same purchase rises by 28%.

🔎 What the calculation shows, item by item

  • Basic-basket rice (R$ 100): zero CBS in both calculations. The law provides a 100% reduction for basic-basket goods, so the rate doesn’t matter.
  • Beer (R$ 150): the Selective Tax is added first (R$ 15), then CBS is calculated on R$ 165. That comes to R$ 14.52 or R$ 18.64.
  • School tuition (R$ 1,200): education gets a 60% reduction in the rate. That comes to R$ 42.24 or R$ 54.24. The program explains the reason in Portuguese and cites the law (Art. 129).
  • Meat with the wrong product code: the program won’t calculate it and warns that the code doesn’t match the classification. On a real invoice, this kind of error would cause the invoice to be rejected.

The purchase total is R$ 1,465.00. Both calculations also show R$ 0.64 in IBS, which is just a 0.1% test in 2027. Run on 23 Nov 2026 using version 1.7.2 of the Federal Revenue program.

What’s happening

November 2026, in order

The tax reform (Complementary Law 214/2025) gradually replaces five consumption taxes with three new ones through 2033. The first major step comes in January. Checked on 24 Nov 2026.

  1. 28 Oct · TCU calculates the rate: 11.3%
    The detailed calculation came to 11.3468%, rounded to 11.3%. This is the benchmark rate: the figure that would make revenue from the new taxes equal revenue from PIS and Cofins.
  2. 9 Nov · Federal Revenue releases the CBS calculation APIs
    The channels through which companies’ systems will submit debits and credits for the new tax.
  3. 10 Nov · The TCU study reaches the Senate
    The Senate sets the benchmark rate by resolution, by 15 December.
  4. 12 and 13 Nov · Order authorizes the code release
    Cotec Order No. 342 is published in the Official Gazette: it releases three programs under a free license (MIT), records each version’s “fingerprint,” and states that there is no support and the code has no force of law.
  5. 16 Nov · Federal Revenue announces open-source code on GitHub
    GitHub is a website where programmers store and share code. Now anyone can see the versions and history, and report errors.
  6. 17 Nov · Finance Ministry cites an average effective rate of 8.5%
    That’s a different figure: the average actually paid after reductions. It doesn’t mean every price will change by 8.5%.
  7. 18 Nov · Senate asks the TCU for its methodology
    The Economic Affairs Committee approved a request to see the studies behind the 11.3%. Experts presented lower estimates.
  8. 19 and 20 Nov · The program already has new versions
    A rounding fix: before, the invoice total could differ from the sum of its items. Proof that live code gets fixed in public.
  9. By 15 Dec · Senate decides the rate
    Once it does, Calculation B (or another one) will no longer be a simulation.
  10. January 2027 · CBS begins
    It replaces PIS and Cofins. IBS enters only as a 0.1% test, and the Selective Tax takes effect.
For you

What this means for your wallet and your work

🛒 If you shop

CBS isn’t an extra tax added on top of everything: it replaces PIS and Cofins, which are already in today’s prices. What changes is the amount, which depends on the rate the Senate sets and on each product’s reductions, such as those for rice and schooling in the calculations above.

🏢 If you sell or run a business

Your invoicing system needs to calculate correctly starting in January. Now you can check: run the same sale through your system and the Federal Revenue program, and see whether the amounts match.

📒 Accountants

An independent second opinion alongside the client’s system, with the reason explained and the article of law cited. Useful for preparing an opinion and explaining to the client why the calculation came out that way.

💻 If you build software

The official reference for testing your own code. You can use the MIT license, but you can’t use the Federal Revenue’s name to claim your product is official.

Common questions

Before you draw conclusions

Will my prices go up by 11.3%?

There’s no way to say that. CBS replaces PIS and Cofins, which are already in the price, and many products and services get a reduction. The calculation varies from case to case, so it’s worth running your own.

Has the rate been decided?

No. As of 24 Nov 2026, the Senate had not yet voted. The deadline is 15 December. Until then, the program marks the 2027 result as “simulated.”

What are CBS, IBS, and the Selective Tax?

CBS: a federal consumption tax replacing PIS and Cofins. IBS: the tax for states and municipalities, replacing ICMS and ISS, which only takes effect in 2029. Selective Tax: an additional tax on products harmful to health or the environment, such as drinks and cigarettes.

Does the program’s result have force of law?

No. The Order itself says the code has no force of law and comes with no support. It’s a strong reference for checking calculations; responsibility for the tax remains with whoever issues the invoice.

Do I need to do anything?

If you only shop, no. If you sell, ask your system provider whether it already calculates CBS for 2027, and check a few sales before January.

How much does it cost?

This page, the Federal Revenue code, and the Open Calculator kit are free. The easiest way to run it uses Claude Code or Codex, which come with a paid Claude or ChatGPT subscription.
