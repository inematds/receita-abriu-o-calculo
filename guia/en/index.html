How much of your money goes to the new tax? Brazil's Federal Revenue Service has opened the calculator

CBS, the federal tax that replaces PIS and Cofins and is included in the price of what you buy, begins in January 2027. In November, Brazil's Federal Revenue Service made the official calculation program public on GitHub (the site where code is stored). We ran the same purchase through it: the result was R$ 56.76 at the example test rate of 8.8%, and R$ 72.88 at 11.3%, the rate recommended by the TCU (Federal Court of Accounts) and still to be voted on by the Senate. In a nutshell

Brazil's Federal Revenue Service has made public the program that calculates the new taxes under the tax reform: CBS (federal, replacing PIS and Cofins), IBS (for states and municipalities), and the Selective Tax (which applies to beverages, cigarettes, and other products). The program has been available for download through the CBS Platform since 2025; the November news is that it was published on GitHub, where every version and fix is visible to everyone, and anyone can run it and check the results. This page explains what happened in November, shows the same purchase calculated with the rate in the Federal Revenue Service's example (8.8%, just a test figure) and the rate recommended by the TCU (11.3%, not yet voted on), and explains what this means for you. Just read on; if you want to run the calculation, find out how at "Check for yourself".

The two calculations

The same purchase, R$ 16.12 apart

We took the example published by the Federal Revenue Service in the program documentation and ran it twice, changing just one number: the CBS rate.

🧾 Calculation A: CBS at 8.8% → R$ 56.76

This is the rate used by the Federal Revenue Service in its documentation example. It is for testing the program; it is not a proposed rate. The total matches the figure in the official documentation.

🧾 Calculation B: CBS at 11.3% → R$ 72.88

This is the reference rate the TCU (Federal Court of Accounts) calculated for 2027 and submitted to the Senate. It has not yet been approved. At this rate, CBS on the same purchase rises by 28%.

🔎 What the calculation shows, item by item

  • Basic food basket rice (R$ 100): zero CBS in both calculations. The law provides a 100% reduction for the basic food basket, so the rate does not matter.
  • Beer (R$ 150): the Selective Tax is applied first (R$ 15), then CBS is calculated on R$ 165. The result is R$ 14.52 or R$ 18.64.
  • School tuition (R$ 1,200): education gets a 60% rate reduction. The result is R$ 42.24 or R$ 54.24. The program explains the reason in Portuguese and cites the law (Art. 129).
  • Meat with the wrong product code: the program does not calculate it and warns that the code does not match the classification. This kind of error can lead to an invoice being rejected; in a direct calculation, an item like this makes the program reject the entire purchase.

The purchase total is R$ 1,465.00. Both calculations also show R$ 0.64 in IBS, which in 2027 is just a 0.1% test. Run on 11/23/2026 with version 1.7.2 of the Federal Revenue Service's program.

January example

A real grocery receipt, what it looks like in January

A purchase of R$ 1,512.15 at a supermarket in RS, on 11/06/2026: 121 items. We ran each line of the receipt through Brazil's Federal Revenue program, as if the purchase were made in January 2027, when CBS begins.

🧾 CBS at 8.8% → R$ 89.14

Using the example's test rate from the Federal Revenue. That's 5.9% of the purchase total.

🧾 CBS at 11.3% → R$ 114.30

Using the rate recommended by the TCU, not yet approved. That's 7.6% of the purchase total. The difference between the two is R$ 25.16.

🔎 What the calculation shows, product by product

  • Basic food basket items have zero CBS: chicken, milk, coffee, sugar, salt, wheat flour, and sagu, totaling R$ 396.04 on the receipt. The program states the reason: zero rate, article 125 of the law.
  • Some foods get a 60% rate reduction: soybean oil, dairy drink, yogurt, and cornstarch (R$ 173.74). The 19 bottles of soybean oil (R$ 132.81) incur R$ 4.67 in CBS at 8.8%, not R$ 11.69.
  • Non-basic items add up: the 81 cans of condensed milk (R$ 404.19) alone incur R$ 35.57 or R$ 45.67 in CBS, and the 81 cartons of cream, R$ 17.75 or R$ 22.79. Gelatin, sauces, mayonnaise, and chocolate drink mix also pay the full rate.
  • The canned-food catch: canned corn and peas and shoestring potatoes may look like "vegetables," but the law's reduction applies only to cooked vegetables with no salt or other ingredients. Canned vegetables contain salt, so they fall under the full rate.

CBS isn't added on top of today's price: it replaces PIS and Cofins, which are already included in the R$ 1,512.15. The calculation shows how much of the purchase becomes CBS, with each tax named item by item.

The receipt doesn't include the products' tax code (NCM): INEMA selected a code for each one and checked its classification in the program's own table. We assumed a supermarket under the standard tax regime (the calculation is different under Simples Nacional) and used the value of each receipt line as the tax base. Both calculations also show R$ 0.70 in IBS, the 0.1% test rate for 2027. This is a simulation, not a tax receipt. Run on 11/24/2026, using version 1.7.2 of the Federal Revenue program; the program input and response are in the kit, at kit/exemplos.

What's happening

November 2026, in order

The tax reform (Complementary Law 214/2025) gradually replaces five consumption taxes with three new ones through 2033. The first major step comes in January. Checked on 11/24/2026.

  1. 11/09 · Federal Revenue Service opens CBS reporting channels
    The channels through which companies' systems will submit debits and credits for the new tax.
  2. 11/10 · The TCU submits the 11.3% rate to the Senate
    The detailed calculation came to 11.3468%. This is the reference rate: the figure that keeps the new revenue equal to what PIS and Cofins bring in. The Senate sets it by resolution, by December 15.
  3. 11/12 and 11/13 · Ordinance authorizes the code release
    Ordinance Cotec No. 342 appears in the Official Gazette: it releases three programs under the MIT License, records each version's "fingerprint," and states that there is no support and the code has no force of law.
  4. 11/16 · Federal Revenue Service announces open-source code on GitHub
    GitHub is the site where programmers store and share code. Now anyone can see the versions and history, and report errors.
  5. 11/17 · The Finance Ministry cites an 8.5% average effective rate
    That is a different figure: the average actually paid after reductions. It does not mean every price changes by 8.5%.
  6. 11/18 · The Senate asks the TCU for its methodology
    The Economic Affairs Committee approved a request to see the studies behind the 11.3% rate.
  7. 11/18 to 11/20 · Both calculators receive new versions
    The regular-regime calculator (version 1.7.2) fixed rounding: before, the invoice total could differ from the sum of its items. The Simples Nacional calculator was released in version 1.0.2. This shows that the code is being corrected in public.
  8. By 12/15 · The Senate decides the rate
    Once decided, Calculation B (or another one) will no longer be a simulation.
  9. January 2027 · CBS begins
    It replaces PIS and Cofins. IBS only comes in as a 0.1% test, and the Selective Tax takes effect.
For you

What this means for your wallet and your work

🛒 Shoppers

CBS is not an extra tax added on top of everything: it replaces PIS and Cofins, which are already included in prices today. What changes is the amount, which depends on the rate set by the Senate and on each product's reductions, such as those for rice and education in the calculations above.

🏢 Sellers and business owners

Your invoicing system needs to calculate correctly starting in January. Now you can check: run the same sale through your system and the Federal Revenue Service's program, then see whether the figures match.

📒 Accountants

An independent second opinion alongside the client's system, with the reason and the relevant article of law spelled out. Useful for an expert opinion and for explaining to clients why the calculation came out that way.

And a warning before rejection: the "blocked" meat in the calculations above has a product code (NCM) that does not match its tax classification—the same error that would cause the invoice to be rejected.

💻 Software developers

The official reference for testing your own code. You can use the MIT License, but you cannot use the Federal Revenue Service's name to claim that your product is official.

Common questions

Before you draw any conclusions

Will my prices go up by 11.3%?

There is no way to say that. CBS replaces PIS and Cofins, which are already included in prices, and many products and services get reductions. The calculation varies from case to case, so it is worth running your own.

Has the rate been decided?

No. As of 11/24/2026, the Senate had not yet voted. The deadline is December 15. Until then, the program marks the 2027 result as "simulated."

What are CBS, IBS, and the Selective Tax?

CBS: a federal consumption tax that replaces PIS and Cofins. IBS: the tax for states and municipalities, replacing ICMS and ISS, which only takes full effect starting in 2029. Selective Tax: an extra tax on products harmful to health or the environment, such as beverages and cigarettes.

Does the program's result have the force of law?

No. The Ordinance itself says the code has no force of law and comes with no support. It is a strong reference for checking calculations; responsibility for the tax remains with whoever issues the invoice.

Do I need to do anything?

If you only shop, no. If you sell, ask your system provider whether it already calculates the 2027 CBS, and check a few sales before January.

How much does it cost?

This page, the Federal Revenue Service's code, and the Open Calculator kit are free. The easiest way to run it uses Claude Code or Codex, which come with a paid Claude or ChatGPT subscription.
