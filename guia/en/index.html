How much of your money goes to the new tax? The Federal Revenue opened the calculator

CBS begins in January 2027: the federal tax replacing PIS and Cofins, built into the price of what you buy. In November, the Federal Revenue opened to the public on GitHub the program it uses to calculate it. We ran the same purchase through it twice, and the results were R$ 56.76 and R$ 72.88. The difference is the rate the Senate still has to decide.

In a sentence

The Federal Revenue opened to everyone the program that calculates the new taxes from the tax reform: CBS (federal, replacing PIS and Cofins), IBS (for states and municipalities), and the Selective Tax (which applies to beverages, cigarettes, and other products). The program had already been available for download through the CBS Platform since 2025; November’s news is that it was opened on GitHub, with every version and fix visible to everyone, so anyone can run it and check the results. This page explains what happened in November, shows the same purchase calculated at the two rates under consideration, and explains what it means for you. Just read on; if you want to run the calculation, see “Try it yourself.”

The two calculations

The same purchase, R$ 16.12 difference

We took the example published by the Federal Revenue in the program documentation and ran it twice, changing just one number: the CBS rate.

🧾 Calculation A: CBS at 8.8% → R$ 56.76

This is the rate the Federal Revenue used in its documentation example. It is for testing the program; it is not a proposed rate. The total matches the figure published in the official documentation.

🧾 Calculation B: CBS at 11.3% → R$ 72.88

This is the reference rate the TCU (Federal Court of Accounts) calculated for 2027 and submitted to the Senate. It has not been approved yet. At this rate, CBS on the same purchase rises by 28%.

🔎 What the calculation shows, item by item

  • Basic-basket rice (R$ 100): zero CBS in both calculations. The law provides a 100% reduction for basic-basket items, so the rate does not matter.
  • Beer (R$ 150): the Selective Tax is applied first (R$ 15), and CBS is calculated on R$ 165. That comes to R$ 14.52 or R$ 18.64.
  • School tuition (R$ 1,200): education gets a 60% rate reduction. That comes to R$ 42.24 or R$ 54.24. The program explains why in Portuguese and cites the law (Art. 129).
  • Meat with the wrong product code: the program does not calculate the tax and warns that the code does not match the classification. This is the kind of error that would get a real invoice rejected.

The purchase total is R$ 1,465.00. Both calculations also show R$ 0.64 in IBS, which in 2027 is just a 0.1% test. Run for real on 23/11/2026 using version 1.7.2 of the Federal Revenue program.

What’s happening

November 2026, in order

The tax reform (Complementary Law 214/2025) gradually replaces five consumption taxes with three new ones through 2033. The first major step comes in January. Checked on 24/11/2026.

  1. Oct 28 · TCU calculates the rate: 11.3%
    The detailed calculation came to 11.3468%, rounded to 11.3%. This is the reference rate: the number that makes revenue from the new taxes match revenue from PIS and Cofins.
  2. Nov 9 · Federal Revenue releases the CBS calculation APIs
    The channels through which companies’ systems will submit debits and credits for the new tax.
  3. Nov 10 · The TCU study reaches the Senate
    The Senate sets the reference rate by resolution, by December 15.
  4. Nov 12 and 13 · Ordinance authorizes the code to be opened
    Ordinance Cotec No. 342 appears in the Federal Official Gazette: it releases three programs under the MIT free software license, records each version’s “fingerprint,” and states that there is no support and that the code has no force of law.
  5. Nov 16 · Federal Revenue announces open-source code on GitHub
    GitHub is a site where programmers store and share code. Now anyone can see the versions and history, and report errors.
  6. Nov 17 · Finance Ministry cites an average effective rate of 8.5%
    That is a different figure: the average actually paid after reductions. It does not mean every price will change by 8.5%.
  7. Nov 18 · Senate asks the TCU for its methodology
    The Economic Affairs Committee approved a request to review the studies behind the 11.3%. Experts presented lower estimates.
  8. Nov 18–20 · Both calculators receive new versions
    The regular-regime calculator (version 1.7.2) fixed rounding: previously, the invoice total could differ from the sum of the items. The Simples Nacional calculator was released in version 1.0.2. Proof that the code is corrected in public.
  9. By Dec 15 · Senate decides the rate
    Once it is announced, calculation B (or another one) will no longer be a simulation.
  10. January 2027 · CBS begins
    It replaces PIS and Cofins. IBS is included only as a 0.1% test, and the Selective Tax takes effect.
For you

What this changes for your wallet and your work

🛒 If you shop

CBS is not an extra tax added on top of everything: it replaces PIS and Cofins, which are already included in prices today. What changes is the size of the bill, depending on the rate set by the Senate and the reductions for each product, such as those for rice and schooling in the calculations above.

🏢 If you sell or run a business

Your invoicing system needs to calculate correctly starting in January. Now you can check: run the same sale through your system and the Federal Revenue program, then see whether the figures match.

📒 Accountants

A second opinion independent of the client’s system, with the reason and the law article stated. Useful for reports and for explaining to clients how the calculation came out.

And a warning before rejection: the “blocked” meat in the calculations above uses a product code (NCM) that does not match the tax classification—the same error that would get the invoice rejected.

💻 If you build software

The official reference for testing your own code. You can use the MIT license, but you cannot use the Federal Revenue’s name to claim your product is official.

Common questions

Before you draw conclusions

Will my prices go up by 11.3%?

There is no way to say that. CBS replaces PIS and Cofins, which are already included in prices, and many products and services get reductions. The calculation varies from case to case, so it is worth running your own.

Has the rate already been decided?

No. As of 24/11/2026, the Senate had not yet voted. The deadline is December 15. Until then, the program marks the 2027 result as “simulated.”

What are CBS, IBS, and the Selective Tax?

CBS: a federal consumption tax that replaces PIS and Cofins. IBS: the tax for states and municipalities that replaces ICMS and ISS; it only takes full effect in 2029. Selective Tax: an extra tax on products that harm health or the environment, such as alcoholic drinks and cigarettes.

Does the program’s result have force of law?

No. The ordinance itself says the code has no force of law and is unsupported. It is a strong reference for checking calculations; responsibility for the tax remains with whoever issues the invoice.

Do I need to do anything?

If you only shop, no. If you sell, ask your system provider whether it already calculates CBS for 2027, and check a few sales before January.
